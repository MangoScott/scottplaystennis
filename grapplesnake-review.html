Grapplesnake Strings Review: Finding My Perfect Setup

March 20, 2025 • 8 min read

After 15 years of string experimentation hell, I've finally found it. The setup that makes me actually want to stay on court longer. The one that doesn't leave my arm screaming or my wallet crying. The holy grail: Grapplesnake Tour Mako in the mains, Tour M8 in the crosses.

But let me back up, because arriving at this combination took a month of testing, countless restrings, and more tennis than my USTA captain probably wanted me playing before sectionals.

The Grapplesnake Lineup

Grapplesnake isn't exactly a household name yet, but they're doing something different. While the big brands iterate on the same formulas year after year, these guys are actually engineering solutions to real problems. Their lineup is refreshingly simple: three strings, each with a clear purpose.

Tour M8: The Control Freak's Dream

This is Grapplesnake's most popular string, and for good reason. Julian Cash just won Wimbledon men's doubles with this in his racket, which should tell you everything about its performance at the highest level.

The Tour M8 is all about precision and control. It's a shaped poly with a unique profile that bites the ball without being overly harsh on the arm. What surprised me most was the tension maintenance — after 10 hours of play, it still felt crisp and responsive. Most polys I've tried turn into wet noodles after half that time.

Best for: Players who live and die by their ability to hit their spots. If you're the type who aims for the lines (and occasionally hits them), this is your string.

Tour Sniper: The ALU Power Alternative

Here's where things get interesting. The Tour Sniper is aluminum-based, designed to compete directly with Luxilon ALU Power and Babolat RPM Blast. But unlike those arm killers, the Sniper actually has some give to it.

The feel reminds me of Tour Bite — that crispy, connected sensation — but with noticeably better comfort. I strung it at 52 lbs in my Blade and played three consecutive days without the usual elbow twinge. That alone makes it worth considering if you're an ALU Power refugee like half the 4.5 players I know.

Best for: Players who want that "pro string" feel without the physical therapy bills.

Tour Mako: The Unicorn

This is the string that changed everything for me. Grapplesnake calls it their power string, but that's underselling it. Yes, it's powerful — surprisingly so for a poly — but the real magic is in the feel.

They've somehow engineered a string that delivers easy power while maintaining control. The surface has a mild texture that grips the ball just enough to keep things predictable. It's round profile should theoretically make it launch balls, but the material composition keeps everything in check. I don't pretend to understand the engineering, but I know what I feel: controlled power that doesn't sacrifice touch.

The Hybrid Discovery

I started by testing each string as a full bed. The M8 gave me surgical precision but left me working too hard for power. The Sniper was comfortable but didn't quite have the pop I wanted. The Mako had the power and feel, but I wanted just a touch more control on my flatter shots.

Then I tried the hybrid: Mako mains, M8 crosses.

It's perfect. And I don't use that word lightly after two decades of chasing string setups.

The Mako mains provide that effortless power and incredible feel. The ball absolutely explodes off the stringbed, but in a predictable way. Meanwhile, the M8 crosses add just enough control to keep my flat forehand from sailing long. The combination of the textured Mako and shaped M8 creates fantastic spin potential without that grabby, inconsistent feel some textured strings have.

What This Setup Does Well

  • Serve: The power from the Mako mains adds an easy 5-10 mph without swinging harder. The M8 crosses help with placement on second serves.
  • Groundstrokes: This is where the magic happens. The feel is pure — you know exactly where the ball is on the strings. Power comes easy, control is always there.
  • Volleys: The softer response of the hybrid gives great touch at net. No more trampolining balls over the baseline.
  • Spin: Not quite Solinco Hyper-G levels, but more than enough for heavy topspin when needed.

Durability & Value

I'm 15 hours into this setup and there's minimal notching. The Mako mains are holding up better than expected — I was worried the power-oriented construction might sacrifice durability, but that hasn't been the case. At around $15-16 per set, Grapplesnake is priced competitively with other performance polys.

Who Should Try This?

If you're a 4.0+ player who:

  • Wants more free power without sacrificing control
  • Values feel and connection to the ball
  • Has been searching for a comfortable poly setup
  • Likes to customize with hybrids

Then you owe it to yourself to try this combination. Or at minimum, grab a set of Tour Mako and see what the fuss is about.

The Verdict

After cycling through ALU Power, RPM Blast, Hyper-G, Tour Bite, and probably a dozen others over the years, I'm done searching. The Mako/M8 hybrid is my setup. Period.

Is it perfect for everyone? Of course not. String preference is deeply personal, influenced by your swing, your racket, your game style. But if you've been on the poly carousel looking for something that delivers both power and control with actual feel, Grapplesnake might just end your search too.

The fact that a relatively unknown company is producing strings that can win at Wimbledon while also working for a 4.5 hack like me? That's impressive engineering.

Final Rating: 9.5/10

The only reason it's not a 10? My backhand still sucks. But I can't blame the strings for that.
